As in the printed first edition, in-chapter illustrations are positioned at the end of chapters, with the exception of a couple of chapters that have line drawings close to the action of the drawing. The illustrations are whenever possible positioned in a chapter in which what is being depicted is mentioned, otherwise they are in the chapter prior or after. The printed edition mixed several full-page illustrations in with the portraits at the end of the book; this edition has positioned those illustrations in the chapters in which they're mentioned as well. This has resulted in a couple of chapters having two illustrations at the end. (The exception is the prickly pear; it is not only not mentioned, it is also not in the first edition’s LoI.)

The endpapers, maps, photographs, and portraits have been separated into their own appendices.

Each of the captions has the last name of the illustrator for that illustration in parentheses; this matches the printed book, where the LOI contained that information.

In the printed book, the epilogue has no title, but is formatted (centered and italicized) in a way that made it obvious. Since this production is not following that formatting, a title was added to make it clear.

Lawrence made it a point not to be consistent in his transliteration of Arabian names and cities (see, e.g., the paragraph at the beginning of Appendix 2), and this edition has kept that inconsistency intact.

The first edition mis-numbered the chapters beginning with chapter 12; there is no chapter 11. This edition corrects that, and so there is one less chapter than in the scans.
